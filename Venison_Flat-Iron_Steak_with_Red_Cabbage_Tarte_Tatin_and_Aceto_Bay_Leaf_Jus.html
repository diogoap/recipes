Product-match notes

AH’s selected wild-meat option is hertenbiefstuk rather than a venison flat-iron or venison-sukade cut. The hertenbiefstuk is therefore the closest match by animal species and preserves the intended game flavour.

The cut-matched fallback is AH Excellent Flat iron steak, approximately 200 g, but that product is beef rather than venison.

Mazzetti is not labelled as a ten-year balsamic. It is the closest premium AH alternative used in this list: the product is rated four leaves and described as having matured for a long period in oak barrels.

Materne is actual redcurrant jelly, but availability may be limited to selected stores. The cranberry-pomegranate spread is the practical online fallback.

Watercress replaces Zorri Cress. It is not identical, but it supplies the necessary fresh, peppery character.

BXC Beef Blend substitute

The Euroma blend contains onion, garlic, paprika, smoked paprika, pepper, chilli and herbs but no added salt, which is why salt and sugar are added separately.

Mix everything first. Apply most of it, then stop once the meat is evenly but lightly coated. It is safer to leave a little unused than to oversalt the venison.

Kamado Preparation

The source cooks the cabbage at 200°C, adds the apple after 15 minutes, cooks it for at least another 15 minutes, and then bakes the pastry indirectly for 15–20 minutes. It reduces the jus de veau with bay to approximately one-third before adding balsamic and butter, and grills the venison to a core temperature of 50°C.

Red Cabbage Tarte Tatin

  1. Remove the coarse outer leaves from the red cabbage and cut out the core.
  2. Slice the cabbage very finely.
  3. Mix it with the Meat Tinsel substitute, berry jelly, the cabbage portion of jus de veau, pepper and salt.
  4. Add the butter and cover the cabbage with baking paper.
  5. Cook indirectly at 200°C for 15 minutes.
  6. Add the peeled, chopped apple.
  7. Continue cooking for at least another 15 minutes, checking the tenderness occasionally.
  8. Remove the baking paper once the cabbage is tender and the liquid has become glossy and syrupy rather than watery.
  9. Let the cabbage cool slightly.
  10. Cover with pricked butter puff pastry and brush the pastry with egg yolk.
  11. Bake indirectly at 200°C for approximately 15–20 minutes, until the pastry is deeply golden.

For the larger quantities, work in manageable batches and maintain approximately the same cabbage depth that worked during your test. More total filling requires more batches, not proportionally longer cooking.

Aceto & Bay Leaf Jus

  1. Put the sauce quantity of jus de veau and the bay leaves in a stainless-steel saucepan.
  2. Reduce until approximately one-third of the starting stock remains.
  3. Add the balsamic vinegar.
  4. Remove the bay leaves.
  5. Whisk in the cold-smoked butter a little at a time.
  6. Stop once the sauce is glossy, emulsified and lightly thickened.
  7. Keep it warm without boiling hard, because aggressive boiling may split the butter emulsion.

Venison

  1. When using a true venison flat-iron or sukade cut, remove the firm central sinew to produce two separate steaks.
  2. Coat the meat lightly with the BXC substitute.
  3. Grill over the cast-iron side, turning regularly.
  4. Cook to the source recipe’s core temperature of approximately 50°C.
  5. Rest the meat for several minutes.
  6. Slice thinly across the grain.
  7. Taste before correcting the seasoning with pepper and salt.

For 10 or 12 people, grill in batches rather than crowding the hot cooking surface.
